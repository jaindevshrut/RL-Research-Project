Dev Shrut Jain (U23AI068)  -  Team Leader
-----------------------------------------
- Designed and implemented Method 2a (preliminary, dropped): a
  Distributional RL backbone with N=64 quantile atoms paired with a
  calibrated linear SVM on a four-dimensional Scherer mapping
  (M2_Dropped/). The configuration mapped Goal_relevance, Conduciveness,
  Power and Fear to Var(Z), E[Z], action-value variance and the
  negative-mass fraction respectively, and routed them onto the
  original Scherer dimensions through a compatibility map; per-condition
  SVM C was calibrated to the human-rater dispersion of Zhang et al.
  (C=0.0032 free, C=0.014 limit) and runs were aggregated over five
  seeds {42, 123, 999, 2024, 7}.
- Documented why Method 2a was dropped: aggregate R^2 collapsed to
  -0.326 against a tabular four-dimensional baseline of 0.501 across
  the five seeds, with aggregate RMSE 40.7% worse than baseline; the
  cause was that the 64-quantile distribution was collapsed to the
  scalar summaries E[Z], Var(Z) and a negative-mass fraction before
  reaching the SVM.
- Designed and implemented Method 2b (the kept QR-DQN configuration in
  the paper): the Quantile-Regression DQN backbone with N=51 atoms
  (src/networks/qr_dqn.py, src/agent_qrdqn.py, src/train_qrdqn.py)
  following Dabney et al., AAAI 2018, on top of the same
  eight-dimensional appraisal vector that Method 1b uses. This fixed
  the structural deficit of Method 2a by sourcing the predictability
  channel directly from the intra-distribution spread of the greedy
  action.
- Derived and coded the asymmetric quantile Huber loss; verified
  numerical correctness via gradient smoke-test.
- Adapted the appraisal extractor's three input statistics
  (predictability, anticipation, power) to the QR-DQN value-head
  representation while leaving the eight appraisal formulas unchanged.
- Ran the 60,000-frame Method 2b training and computed the comparison
  metrics reported in row Exp 3 of the master conclusion table.


Sweta Rana (U23AI065)
---------------------
- Designed and implemented Method 1a (preliminary, dropped): a vanilla
  Deep Q-Network paired with a six-dimensional appraisal vector and a
  ten-head MLP ensemble classifier (M1_Dropped/Prj/Appraisal_RL/).
  The six dimensions consisted of the four original Scherer channels
  plus intrinsic unpredictability (transition entropy
  H = -Sum p_i log p_i / log n) and normative significance
  (TD-error z-score |delta - mean(delta)| / (sigma_delta + epsilon)).
- Documented why Method 1a was dropped: the MLP ensemble offered no
  consistent improvement over a single calibrated SVM on the
  Scherer-style four-experiment evaluation
  (R^2 = {0.555, 0.782, 0.021, 0.483} vs SVM {0.505, 0.762, 0.034,
  0.656}); the Exp3_Free R^2 collapsed to near-zero under both
  classifiers, and top-1 emotion classification reached only 4/7
  (57.1%) on Exp1_2 and 3/4 (75%) on Exp3 -- substantially below the
  headline accuracies of the eight-dimensional extension.
- Designed and implemented Method 1b (the kept and headline
  configuration in the paper): the Dueling Double DQN with a bootstrap
  ensemble of K=3 value heads (src/networks/dueling_dqn.py,
  src/agent.py, src/train.py). This fixed both deficits of Method 1a
  by extending the appraisal vector from six to eight dimensions with
  channels read from genuinely disjoint statistics, and by upgrading
  the backbone so those statistics become available as named outputs.
- Implemented the eight-dimensional appraisal extractor
  (src/appraisal/extractor.py): the four paper-faithful CPM channels
  plus the four newly proposed channels (predictability, anticipation,
  urgency, familiarity).
- Implemented the analysis pipeline: correlation matrix, per-dimension
  variance-inflation factors, effective-rank computation, permutation
  importance, headline R^2 / RMSE / accuracy
  (analysis/correlation_analysis.py, analysis/explainability.py,
  analysis/rmse_r2.py, analysis/generate_figures.py).
- Wrote the entire research paper (paper/conference_101719.tex):
  abstract, introduction, related work, background, methodology
  (covering all four methods: 1a, 1b, 2a, 2b), mathematical modelling,
  implementation details, experimental setup, results, discussion,
  conclusion and future-scope sections.
- Produced all figures and tables in the paper from the actual run
  artefacts; ran every plagiarism / AI-detection humanisation pass on
  the manuscript.
- Wrote the README and prepared the reproducibility package.

All listed contributions are factual descriptions of the work done by
each author in the period leading to the submission. The order of
authorship on the paper title page (Dev, Sweta, Krish) reflects the
joint decision of the team.

The paper documents four methods in total. The two preliminary
configurations (Method 1a by Sweta, Method 2a by Dev) are reported
transparently in the methodology section together with the empirical
evidence that motivated their replacement; the two retained
configurations (Method 1b by Sweta, the headline; Method 2b by Dev,
the parallel comparison) are evaluated head-to-head in the results
section.
